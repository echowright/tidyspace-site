How to grant or remove access on many Confluence pages at once

A person joins a team, or leaves one. Here is how to update page restrictions across many pages in one previewed job.

The problem

Page restrictions are set page by page in the Confluence interface. When a team changes, an administrator can face hundreds of pages. The risk is also real: a wrong change can make a page inaccessible to the people who need it.

Steps with Tidyspace

You need to be a Confluence administrator and have Tidyspace installed.

  1. Open Confluence administration, then Apps, then Tidyspace.
  2. Choose Restrictions, then Grant access (Can view or Can edit) or Remove access.
  3. Pick up to 10 users and groups.
  4. Select the content: spaces, pages and folders in the tree, a label, a modification date, or a CQL query.
  5. Click Prepare the preview.

The preview shows before and after

For the first items, the preview shows the access before and after the job and the effect on each page. Nothing is written until you click Run.

Two safeguards

  • No lock-out. A removal that would leave only the app able to view or edit a page is refused and reported as “Left unchanged”.
  • No widening of your rights. Tidyspace only touches pages you can see and edit yourself, so it cannot be used to give yourself access to a page that excludes you.

The app itself is added with “Can edit” on every restriction it sets, because Confluence refuses a restriction that excludes the app setting it. More in documentation, Restrictions.

After the job

The panel lists failed and skipped pages, and the CSV export contains all of them, which is useful as a record of what changed. A job can be resumed if it was interrupted, and an old job can be re-run as a new preview.
